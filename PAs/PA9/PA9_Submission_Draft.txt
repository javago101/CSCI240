QUESTION 2: Do the collected results for all exercises seem reasonable? Explain why or why not?

Yes, the results are reasonable and follow Big-O complexity expectations:

1. Complexity: Insertion Sort (O(n^2)) is highly efficient for small or sorted 
   lists (only 9 comparisons for the sorted 1k set), but its performance 
   degrades quickly for larger random sets.
   
2. Scalability: Merge Sort and Quick Sort (O(n log n)) are much more efficient 
   for large data. Quick Sort was the fastest on the 100k dataset (9ms) because 
   it operates in-place, whereas Merge Sort requires auxiliary memory and 
   significantly more data moves (3.3M vs 1.3M).

3. Observation: The time taken to count inversions (5ms) was longer than 
   the time taken to sort the same 1k list (3ms), confirming that O(n log n) 
   sorting is more efficient than O(n^2) verification for this data size.
